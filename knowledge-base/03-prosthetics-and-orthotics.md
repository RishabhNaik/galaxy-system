# 03 - Prosthetics, Orthotics & Manufacturing

## Introduction
Galaxy System provides custom-designed prosthetic limbs and orthotic devices to restore functional independence, mobility, and quality of life. Neither type of device is mass-produced — each is individually tailored based on the patient's anatomy, residual limb condition, lifestyle, occupation, and rehabilitation goals.

> **Note:** A **prosthesis** *replaces* a missing limb. An **orthosis** *supports* a limb that is present but needs assistance.

> **Common Misconception:** Many people believe universal prosthetics or orthotics are readily available off-the-shelf. This is incorrect — every device must be **customized** to the individual. Additionally, receiving a prosthesis is not the end; the user must go through a **practice and training period** of several days before being able to use it effectively in daily life.

## Prosthetics (Artificial Limbs)
Prostheses are prescribed following amputation or for congenital limb differences.

### Lower Limb Prostheses
| Type | Indication | Key Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| **Partial Foot** | Partial foot amputation (ankle preserved) | Pressure redistribution, preserves walking |
| **Syme** | Foot removed, heel pad preserved | End-bearing stability |
| **Transtibial (Below-Knee)** | Amputation below the knee (knee preserved) | Better gait, lower energy use, easier rehab |
| **Knee Disarticulation** | Amputation through the knee joint | Full femur length, good end-bearing |
| **Transfemoral (Above-Knee)** | Amputation above the knee | Requires prosthetic knee mechanism |
| **Hip Disarticulation** | Entire lower limb lost at hip | Most complex; requires extensive training |

### Upper Limb Prostheses
- **Partial Hand / Finger:** Restores grip support and appearance.
- **Transradial (Below-Elbow):** Elbow preserved; better functional control and dexterity.
- **Transhumeral (Above-Elbow):** Both elbow and hand must be replaced; complex rehabilitation.
- **Shoulder Disarticulation:** Entire upper limb lost; specialized suspension required.

### Prosthetic System Types
- **Passive (Cosmetic):** Restores appearance; lightweight and low maintenance but limited function.
- **Body-Powered:** Cables and harness operated by body movement; durable, reliable, good sensory feedback.
- **Myoelectric:** Powered by electrical muscle signals (EMG) in the residual limb; better cosmesis and grip patterns, but requires training and more maintenance.

### Key Components
- **Socket:** Most critical component; connects prosthesis to residual limb. Must distribute pressure evenly.
- **Liner:** Cushions the residual limb (typically silicone, gel, or thermoplastic elastomer); improves comfort and suspension.
- **Suspension System:** Keeps prosthesis securely attached. Methods: suction, pin-lock, vacuum, sleeve, or belt.
- **Pylon:** Internal structural frame (aluminium, titanium, or carbon fibre) connecting socket to foot.
- **Prosthetic Knee/Foot:** Ranges from simple mechanical to hydraulic, pneumatic, or microprocessor-controlled (for above-knee); foot type selected based on activity level and body weight.

### Construction: Endoskeletal vs. Exoskeletal
- **Endoskeletal (Modular):** Internal pylon frame with a cosmetic foam cover. Preferred for its **lighter weight, easier adjustability, and modular component compatibility**.
- **Exoskeletal:** Rigid outer shell provides both structure and shape. Durable but harder to adjust after fabrication.

## Orthotics (Supportive Braces)
Orthoses support, stabilize, align, protect, or correct a body part when the limb is present. Conditions treated include post-polio paralysis, stroke, cerebral palsy, spinal cord injury, muscular dystrophy, and arthritis.

### Lower Limb Orthoses
- **Foot Orthoses (FO):** Custom shoe inserts for flat feet, high arches, plantar fasciitis, heel pain, and diabetic foot conditions.
- **Ankle Foot Orthoses (AFO):** Most commonly prescribed lower-limb orthosis. Extends from below-knee to foot. Used for foot drop, stroke, cerebral palsy, and post-polio paralysis. Types: rigid, articulated, posterior leaf spring, ground reaction, custom dynamic.
- **Knee Orthoses (KO):** Stabilizes the knee for ligament injuries (ACL/PCL/MCL/LCL), osteoarthritis, and sports injuries.
- **Knee-Ankle-Foot Orthoses (KAFO):** Extends from thigh to foot. Prescribed when an AFO alone cannot provide adequate stability — for post-polio paralysis, spinal cord injury, or severe muscle weakness.

### Spinal Orthoses
- **CO (Cervical):** Supports/limits neck movement after injury or surgery.
- **LSO (Lumbar-Sacral):** For lower back pain, lumbar instability, post-operative recovery.
- **TLSO (Thoraco-Lumbar-Sacral):** For scoliosis, fractures, or spinal deformities.

### Upper Limb Orthoses
- **Wrist Hand Orthosis (WHO):** Stabilizes the wrist for carpal tunnel, tendon injuries, stroke rehab, and nerve injuries.
- **Elbow Orthosis:** For fractures, ligament repairs, or contracture management.
- **Shoulder Orthosis:** For instability, injury, or neurological impairment.
- **Hand/Finger Splints:** For contracture prevention, tendon rehabilitation, and functional positioning.

## Manufacturing & Workshop Workflow
1. **Clinical Assessment & Measurements:** Residual limb/body part dimensions, joint centres, and bone landmarks captured via plaster casting or digital scanning.
2. **Positive Model Preparation:** Negative cast converted to a positive model; technicians modify it to reduce pressure points and optimize biomechanics.
3. **Device Design:** Socket type, joint configuration, suspension method, material selection, and component choice finalized.
4. **Fabrication:** Thermoforming, vacuum forming, lamination, or heat moulding; manual finishing to achieve strength with minimal weight.
5. **Trial Fitting & Dynamic Alignment:** Patient attends fitting; walking pattern, gait symmetry, joint stability, and comfort are assessed and fine-tuned.
6. **Final Finishing & Quality Control:** Surface smoothing, strap adjustment, structural integrity check, and dimensional accuracy verification before delivery.

## Rehabilitation & Follow-Up Care
Receiving a device is the beginning — not the end — of rehabilitation. Galaxy System provides education on:
- Correct donning/doffing technique and wearing schedule.
- Skin inspection after use (pressure sores are a key risk).
- Cleaning procedures and storage.
- Activity precautions and when to seek adjustments.

**Follow-up appointments** are essential because the residual limb shape, muscle strength, and gait change over time. Regular reviews help maintain alignment, replace worn components, prevent complications, and ensure the device continues to support the individual's goals.
