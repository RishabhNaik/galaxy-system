# 02 - Adaptive Vehicle Modifications

## Introduction & Philosophy
Adaptive vehicle modification engineers a standard vehicle so it can be safely and legally operated by a person with a physical disability. The goal is **not** to redesign the vehicle, but to adapt its controls to the driver's physical abilities while maintaining safety, reliability, and RTO compliance.

**Core Principles:**
- **Individual-Centred Design:** Customized after a thorough assessment of the driver's abilities, not just their diagnosis.
- **Safety First & Legal Compliance:** RTO compliance and safety are prioritized over convenience. A modification is never recommended simply because it is available.
- **Preserve Vehicle Integrity:** Changes are designed to be as non-permanent as possible — protecting the vehicle's safety systems, manufacturer standards, and resale value.

## Customer Assessment Workflow
A systematic evaluation is conducted before any recommendation is made:
1. **Medical & Physical Assessment:** Reviewing disability type, muscle strength, grip, joint mobility, coordination, sitting balance, and transfer ability.
2. **Functional & Lifestyle Assessment:** Evaluating reaction speed, ability to operate emergency braking, daily travel distance, occupation, and driving frequency.
3. **Vehicle Selection Guidance:** Advising on a suitable vehicle (manual vs. automatic, cabin space, seat height, pedal layout) **before purchase** — this reduces modification costs and improves long-term usability.

> **⚠️ Common Pitfall:** Many customers rely heavily on others (family, friends) when choosing a vehicle and adaptive devices, rather than basing the choice on their own specific functional need. Since every individual's disability is different, the assessment must be personal — not generalized.

> **Most Common Customer Questions:** *"Can I drive a car?"* and *"Which car should I get?"* — Galaxy System addresses both through the assessment and vehicle selection process.

## Key Adaptive Solutions
1. **Push-Pull Hand Control:** For bilateral lower limb disabilities. **Push** the lever to **brake**; **pull** to **accelerate**. Works mechanically via linkages to the pedals. Suitable for: post-polio paralysis, spinal cord injury, bilateral amputation.
2. **Left Foot Accelerator:** A secondary accelerator pedal installed to the **left of the brake pedal** for individuals with right-leg limitations (e.g., right-leg polio, amputation). The left foot handles both the accelerator and brake, using natural leg movement.
3. **Ring Accelerator & Brake Lever:** A steering-wheel-mounted ring that controls acceleration, paired with a separate hand-operated brake lever. Designed for **automatic transmission** vehicles; suitable for wheelchair users and those with complete lower limb paralysis.
4. **Automatic Clutch System:** Automates clutch engagement while retaining manual gear shifting, for individuals with **left-leg** disabilities. A cost-effective alternative to full transmission conversion.
5. **Steering Wheel Transmitter:** Mounts secondary controls (indicators, horn, headlights, wipers, hazard lights) **on the steering wheel** so a driver with one functional upper limb never needs to remove their hand from the wheel.
6. **Person Lift:** Assists wheelchair users during vehicle entry/exit, reducing physical effort and caregiver dependency. For: spinal cord injury, muscular dystrophy.
7. **Tip-Up Plate:** A specialized vehicle entry/exit modification for wheelchair users that enables them to transfer in and out of the vehicle with greater ease. Different from a standard Person Lift — it is a platform-based solution fitted to the vehicle door area.
8. **Pedal Extensions:** Mechanical devices that bring pedals closer for drivers of **short stature** or reduced leg reach (e.g., dwarfism, congenital limb differences).

*Note: Combination systems are common — e.g., Push-Pull Hand Control + Automatic Clutch, Left Foot Accelerator + Steering Transmitter, Ring Accelerator + Person Lift.*

## Installation & Quality Assurance
Installation follows a structured engineering workflow: vehicle inspection → component preparation → installation using approved methods → functional adjustment for the individual's body dimensions and posture → road testing.
- **Quality Control:** Verifies mechanical integrity, full control travel, smooth operation, emergency braking response, steering clearance, and driver ergonomics.
- **Customer Training:** Essential step — drivers are familiarized with the new controls before independent driving. Includes starting/stopping, emergency braking, reversing, and gear shifting.
- **Maintenance & Support:** Periodic servicing of linkages, fasteners, cables, and moving parts. Worn components should be replaced promptly. Galaxy System provides ongoing repairs and adjustments.

## RTO Compliance & Government Benefits
Galaxy System guides customers through legal compliance and RTO documentation.
- **Registration & Inspections:** Vehicle modifications may require updating RTO records. Requirements vary by state; customers are advised to verify with their local RTO.
- **Driving Licence:** Individuals with disabilities may need a medical evaluation and driving assessment; the licence may include endorsements reflecting the adaptive controls used.
- **Government Benefits (subject to eligibility):**
  - **Road Tax Exemption:** Many states offer road tax concessions for eligible persons with disabilities.
  - **Insurance Concessions:** Some insurers offer premium concessions for adapted vehicles.
  - **FASTag Benefits:** Eligible adapted vehicles may qualify for FASTag-related exemptions.
  - **Govt. Employee Reimbursement:** Eligible government employees may receive departmental reimbursement for adaptive modifications.
- **Insurance:** Customers must inform their insurer about adaptive modifications to maintain policy validity. Retaining installation invoices and records is essential.
